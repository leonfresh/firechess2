## Key Facts
- **Event**: Linares 1998, Round 10, March 4
- **Players**: Veselin Topalov (White, 2740) vs Alexei Shirov (Black, 2710)
- **Opening**: Grünfeld Defense, Exchange Variation (D85)
- **Result**: 0-1 (Black wins on move 53)
- **The famous move**: 47...Bh3!! — a bishop sacrifice on a square where two pawns can capture it, yet neither capture wins

## Why It's Famous
1. The move looks like a losing blunder — giving away a bishop for nothing
2. Computers at the time evaluated it as losing for Black
3. Even Kasparov, watching ringside, couldn't immediately explain it
4. It involves deep zugzwang, king activity, and opposite-colored bishop dynamics
5. Often called "the greatest endgame move in chess history"

## Key Variations
- **48.gxh3**: The main line. Black's king goes Kf5-Ke4, reaching e4 before White's king can get to e3. The d-pawn queens.
- **48.Kf2**: Declining the sacrifice. Then 48...Kf5 49.Ke3 Bxg2 wins a clean pawn and Black's three connected passers are unstoppable.

## Key Themes
- Zugzwang in the endgame
- King activity vs material
- Opposite-colored bishops (the defender's bishop can't control the right squares)
- Human creativity vs computer evaluation
- The "restriction" theme: the bishop on h3 ties White's king to the g-pawn
